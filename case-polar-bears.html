Case studies/Polar bears
Greenland

Polar bears.

Predation, climate change and food security

Exploring changing encounters between polar bears and people, and their connections to hunting, safety and food security.

In Ittoqqortoormiit, encounters with hungry polar bears are a concern for local residents. Community members connect these encounters to changing sea ice, bear populations and wildlife management.

Polar bears have long been sources of food and skins in Indigenous communities. Changes in hunting opportunities and environmental conditions raise questions about safety, food security and the future of local hunting practices.

Working with hunters, families and local wildlife-management practitioners, this case investigates how different understandings of predation and sustainability intersect with climate change, colonial histories and geopolitical interests.

The project overview also records concerns that bears moving onto land may increasingly prey on muskoxen. This connects the polar bear and muskox cases through questions of predation, hunting and food security.

How we work

Collaborative research with hunters, families and wildlife-management practitioners.
