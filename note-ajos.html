Field story · Finland

People, seals and salmon in Ajos

On fishing boats in the Bothnian Bay, the fieldwork team learns how people negotiate everyday relationships with seals and salmon.

Spring–summer 2026Adapted from Florian Stammler’s Finland fieldwork update

Learning at sea

In the small port of Ajos, fishing and seal hunting belong to the histories of many of the same families. Spring and summer fieldwork brought the Rovaniemi team into these overlapping practices, learning with hosts through time spent together on the sea.

Technology in a multispecies relationship

Fishers demonstrated large traps mounted on inflatable runners. A compressor raises the traps so that the catch can be retrieved. The team also encountered acoustic deterrent devices near traps, intended to discourage seals from approaching the catch.

For the researchers, these technologies opened questions about the relationship between people, fish and seals. They approached the equipment as part of how fishers negotiate access to a shared marine environment, while recording concerns about its effects on animals.

Participation as a research method

The team joined fishing and processing activities, learning through practical participation as well as conversation. Preparing and sharing food also revealed seasonal traditions, including a midsummer meal of salmon heads.

These encounters show why the project works across species and practices: fishing, hunting, technology, food and knowledge transmission cannot be understood in isolation from one another.

Making space for people, fish and seals

The fieldwork account describes traps designed to prevent seals becoming entangled in the mesh. Hosts explain acoustic deterrents as a way of negotiating fishing space: particular traps are places where people catch salmon while seals can feed elsewhere. The account also records concerns about the devices’ effects on seals.

Learning through making

Researchers joined the cleaning, salting and freezing of salmon roe. Florian relates this practical participation to a making approach, citing Ingold (2013) in the source account. Working together opened conversations that went beyond formal interviews.
